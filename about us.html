About the World Health Organization Uganda

Working together to improve health and well-being in Uganda.

The World Health Organization (WHO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for promoting and protecting the health of people around the world. Established on 7 April 1948, WHO works with governments and other partners to improve health services, prevent diseases and respond to public health emergencies.

In Uganda, WHO provides technical guidance and support to the government, particularly the Ministry of Health, and other health partners. Its work contributes to strengthening the country's health system, improving disease surveillance, supporting immunization programmes and promoting access to quality healthcare for all people.

Our Mission

WHO's mission is to promote health, keep the world safe and serve vulnerable populations. In Uganda, this involves supporting efforts to prevent illness, reduce avoidable deaths, improve the quality of healthcare and ensure that communities receive reliable health information and essential health services.

Our Vision

WHO works towards a world in which all people can attain the highest possible level of health. In Uganda, this vision supports a healthier population where individuals and families can access essential health services without being left behind because of poverty, location or other barriers.

Our Key Areas of Work

1. Disease Prevention and Control: WHO supports efforts to prevent and control diseases such as malaria, tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS, cholera and measles. It also assists in disease surveillance and the early identification of outbreaks.

2. Emergency Preparedness and Response: WHO works with national authorities and health partners to prepare for and respond to epidemics and other public health emergencies. This support helps improve coordination and protect communities during health crises.

3. Maternal and Child Health: WHO promotes safe pregnancy, quality childbirth services, newborn care, breastfeeding and childhood immunization. These efforts help mothers and children live healthier lives.

4. Health System Strengthening: WHO provides technical guidance to improve health policies, health worker training, essential medicines, health information systems and the delivery of health services.

5. Health Promotion: WHO encourages communities to practise good hygiene, eat nutritious food, engage in physical activity and adopt healthy lifestyles. It also promotes public awareness of important health issues.

Working with Partners

WHO works with the Ugandan government, health professionals, United Nations agencies, development partners and local communities. Cooperation among these groups helps address public health challenges, share knowledge and improve the delivery of healthcare services throughout the country.

Our Commitment to Uganda

WHO remains committed to supporting Uganda's efforts to improve public health, strengthen healthcare systems and protect people from preventable diseases. Through technical assistance, evidence-based guidance and collaboration with health partners, WHO contributes to building healthier and more resilient communities.
